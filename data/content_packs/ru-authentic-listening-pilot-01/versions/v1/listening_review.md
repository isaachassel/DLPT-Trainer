### 1. Аделина Сотникова — Sport / self-description

Recording date: 2014-03-01 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:17.74 · target from transcript: 1+

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Adelina_Sotnikova_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/94/Adelina_Sotnikova_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=610527981)

**Why this level:** Familiar adjectives and an explicit contrast between daily life and performing. Repeated я чувствую себя supports comprehension; the figurative description raises the second item’s demand.

**Teacher listening check:** Check the fast delivery suggested by the caption-based pace. Do not test the unusual поделю лед phrase without confirming the sound and interpretation. Two questions are enough for this short, narrow excerpt.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

На льду я чувствую себя по-другому, чем в жизни. В жизни я такая тихая, скромная, спокойная девочка, а, когда я выхожу на лед, я чувствую себя совсем другой. Я чувствую себя, можно сказать, королевой на льду, потому что я никогда не… поделю лед, потому что это уже моё, это другие ощущения.

**Questions**

**1.1. How does the speaker contrast her everyday self with her experience on the ice?**

- **A.** She is sociable in everyday life but withdrawn on the ice.
- **B.** She is reserved in everyday life but feels commanding on the ice.
- **C.** She feels equally calm and modest in both settings.
- **D.** She is confident in everyday life but feels out of place on the ice.

**1.2. How does she describe herself in ordinary life?**

- **A.** Competitive and demanding.
- **B.** Talkative and energetic.
- **C.** Anxious and indecisive.
- **D.** Quiet, modest, and calm.

**Answer key and evidence**

**1.1: B — contrast**

The answer combines both sides of the contrast; it does not require knowing who the athlete is.

- c1 · 00:00.00–00:02.92: «На льду я чувствую себя по-другому, чем в жизни.»
- c2 · 00:02.96–00:06.11: «В жизни я такая тихая, скромная, спокойная девочка,»
- c3 · 00:06.14–00:08.76: «а, когда я выхожу на лед, я чувствую себя совсем другой.»
- c4 · 00:08.78–00:11.73: «Я чувствую себя, можно сказать, королевой на льду, потому что»

- **A:** This reverses the everyday description and does not match the image of a queen on the ice.
- **B:** Quiet, modest, and calm contrast with feeling like a queen in her own domain.
- **C:** She explicitly says she feels different when she goes onto the ice.
- **D:** The passage places the reserved description in everyday life and a sense of ownership on the ice.

**1.2: D — explicit_detail**

This lower-demand item checks a concrete description rather than knowledge of skating.

- c2 · 00:02.96–00:06.11: «В жизни я такая тихая, скромная, спокойная девочка,»

- **A:** These traits are not her stated everyday self-description.
- **B:** She instead calls herself quiet and calm.
- **C:** Neither trait is stated; quiet does not mean anxious.
- **D:** These directly paraphrase тихая, скромная, спокойная.

### 2. Дмитрий Шпаро — Creative work / thinking habits

Recording date: 2014-07-26 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:32.09 · target from transcript: 2

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dmitrij_Shparo_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/Dmitrij_Shparo_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=633847801)

**Why this level:** A connected personal explanation compares writing with mathematical problem-solving and ends with a conditional intention. Repetitions and embedded clauses require following an unfinished spoken thought.

**Teacher listening check:** Confirm that the listener can follow the long opening sentence at natural speed. Knowledge of how to prove a theorem is unnecessary; the comparison is stated.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

Я все-таки много пишу, и вот когда я пишу, когда я занимаюсь творчеством, связанным с тем, что ты мыслишь и что-то такое записываешь, а потом это исправляешь, исправляешь, исправляешь, исправляешь… И это очень похоже на то, как я доказывал теоремы. Я доказывал теоремы, там, в трамвае, в метро, и сейчас всё это продолжается. Я ложусь спать и составляю те предложения, которые на следующее утро, если не забуду, постараюсь написать.

**Questions**

**2.1. What similarity does the speaker draw between writing and proving theorems?**

- **A.** Both involve thinking through ideas and working them over.
- **B.** Both must be completed at a desk before going to bed.
- **C.** Both depend on copying a solution that already exists.
- **D.** Both have become activities that he no longer pursues.

**2.2. What does he describe doing as he goes to bed?**

- **A.** Reading the sentences he wrote that morning.
- **B.** Writing down solutions to other people’s theorems.
- **C.** Composing sentences in his mind for possible use the next morning.
- **D.** Correcting a manuscript with another writer.

**2.3. What qualification does he place on writing those sentences the next morning?**

- **A.** He will do so only while traveling on the metro.
- **B.** He will try to write them if he has not forgotten them.
- **C.** He is certain to write every sentence exactly as planned.
- **D.** He will first ask someone else to check their grammar.

**Answer key and evidence**

**2.1: A — main_idea**

Follow the comparison introduced by похоже на то, как; it concerns a mental process, not a shared workplace.

- c1 · 00:00.00–00:05.43: «Я все-таки много пишу, и вот когда я пишу,»
- c3 · 00:07.80–00:12.41: «связанным с тем, что ты мыслишь и что-то такое записываешь,»
- c4 · 00:12.43–00:17.78: «а потом это исправляешь, исправляешь, исправляешь, исправляешь…»
- c5 · 00:18.77–00:21.41: «И это очень похоже на то, как я доказывал теоремы.»

- **A:** He links thinking, recording, and repeated correction in writing to his earlier theorem work.
- **B:** He recalls proving theorems on public transport and composing sentences at bedtime.
- **C:** No copying or external model is described.
- **D:** He says he writes a lot and that the thinking process continues.

**2.2: C — detail_paraphrase**

Distinguish mentally composing sentences now from writing them down later.

- c7 · 00:26.38–00:30.10: «Я ложусь спать и составляю те предложения, которые на следующее утро,»
- c8 · 00:30.14–00:32.09: «если не забуду, постараюсь написать.»

- **A:** He is forming sentences for the following morning, not reading a completed text.
- **B:** The theorem example concerns his earlier activity; this final example concerns sentences.
- **C:** He forms sentences at bedtime and hopes to write them the next morning if he remembers.
- **D:** No collaborator is mentioned.

**2.3: B — conditional_intention**

A plan qualified by memory and effort is not a guaranteed result.

- c8 · 00:30.14–00:32.09: «если не забуду, постараюсь написать.»

- **A:** The metro belongs to the earlier theorem example.
- **B:** This preserves both если не забуду and постараюсь.
- **C:** The conditional and the verb of trying rule out certainty.
- **D:** No such check is mentioned.

### 3. Анатолий Засов — Science / childhood curiosity

Recording date: 2014-08-23 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:30.81 · target from transcript: 2

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anatolij_Zasov_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8d/Anatolij_Zasov_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=630394118)

**Why this level:** The speaker explains a familiar social process using abstract vocabulary, alternative causes, and a qualifying conclusion. No astronomy facts are needed.

**Teacher listening check:** Review whether the abstract vocabulary pushes the clip toward 2+ for the target group. The question about lost interest should preserve the alternatives rather than assert both occur in every case.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

Люди с детства сталкиваются с астрономическими явлениями, и им интересно понять, что это такое. А взрослые, как правило, не могут ответить на их вопросы. Тогда начинают искать, начинают интересоваться. А потом со временем либо взрослые гасят всякую любознательность, либо другие интересы настолько перебивают, что уже люди забывают, что мы часть большого мира, большой Вселенной, и уже занимаются более прагматичными вещами. Но любителей-то астрономии всегда много и было, и есть.

**Questions**

**3.1. What initially draws children toward astronomy in the speaker’s account?**

- **A.** Encountering phenomena that they want to understand.
- **B.** Receiving complete explanations from adults.
- **C.** Discovering that astronomy offers practical career benefits.
- **D.** Being required to memorize scientific terminology.

**3.2. What reasons does he give for interest in astronomy fading?**

- **A.** Children receive so many answers that there is nothing left to discover.
- **B.** Astronomical phenomena become less visible as people grow older.
- **C.** Astronomy clubs stop accepting people once they become adults.
- **D.** Adults may discourage curiosity, or other interests may crowd it out.

**3.3. What does the speaker emphasize at the end?**

- **A.** Only professional scientists remain interested in astronomy.
- **B.** Interest in astronomy has disappeared from society.
- **C.** There have nevertheless always been many astronomy enthusiasts.
- **D.** Adults have recently become able to answer all children’s questions.

**Answer key and evidence**

**3.1: A — explicit_cause**

The starting point is curiosity about observed phenomena, not formal study or a career plan.

- c1 · 00:00.00–00:02.69: «Люди с детства сталкиваются с астрономическими явлениями,»
- c2 · 00:02.71–00:04.83: «и им интересно понять, что это такое.»
- c3 · 00:05.32–00:08.22: «А взрослые, как правило, не могут ответить на их вопросы.»

- **A:** The opening connects exposure to astronomical phenomena with wanting to know what they are.
- **B:** The speaker says adults generally cannot answer their questions.
- **C:** Practical concerns appear later as competing interests, not the initial attraction.
- **D:** No memorization requirement is described.

**3.2: D — alternative_causes**

The correct choice retains the alternatives and avoids treating either cause as universal.

- c5 · 00:10.77–00:15.24: «А потом со временем либо взрослые гасят всякую любознательность,»
- c6 · 00:15.27–00:20.77: «либо другие интересы настолько перебивают, что уже люди забывают,»
- c8 · 00:23.88–00:26.84: «и уже занимаются более прагматичными вещами.»

- **A:** The excerpt begins with unanswered questions, not complete explanations.
- **B:** The explanation concerns social and personal interests, not visibility.
- **C:** No admission rule or age restriction is mentioned.
- **D:** The two alternatives follow либо ... либо.

**3.3: C — qualifying_conclusion**

The final contrast prevents an overly pessimistic interpretation of the earlier generalization.

- c9 · 00:26.88–00:30.79: «Но любителей-то астрономии всегда много и было, и есть.»

- **A:** Любителей means enthusiasts; the conclusion is not limited to professionals.
- **B:** This contradicts the closing statement that there have always been many enthusiasts.
- **C:** The final Но qualifies the preceding account of fading curiosity.
- **D:** No such improvement is stated.

### 4. Александр Лазуткин — Spaceflight / response to danger

Recording date: 2012-10-01 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:38.01 · target from transcript: 2

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aleksandr_Lazutkin_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/Aleksandr_Lazutkin_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=628701793)

**Why this level:** A concrete account of emergency action is contrasted with later emotional processing. Hypotheticals, false starts, and the elliptical ending increase the inference demand.

**Teacher listening check:** Check that the fire/collision references and the final counterfactual are intelligible. Keep the inference narrow: he fears a fatal alternative, without specifying an accident outcome beyond what he says.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

Если ты начнёшь осознавать степень опасности, ты подумаешь: а вообще мне стоит лететь туда или не стоит, стоит так делать или не стоит. Допустим, когда у нас какие-то там ситуации были страшные, тот же пожар или столкновение со станцией, когда наступал момент, то там кидаешься пожар тушить — ты даже не думаешь, насколько это опасно — или дырочку закрываешь. А потом, когда ты осмысливаешь ситуацию, уже вроде всё улеглось, и тебе становится потом страшно, думаешь: ёлки-палки, вот если бы я бы вот... по-другому пошло бы всё, то я бы уже, может быть, и не думал бы.

**Questions**

**4.1. When does the speaker say fear can become prominent?**

- **A.** Before every task, preventing him from taking action.
- **B.** After the immediate crisis, when he reflects on what happened.
- **C.** Only after someone else explains the situation to him.
- **D.** Only while deciding whether to begin his first spaceflight.

**4.2. What does he describe doing during an emergency?**

- **A.** Trying to extinguish a fire or close a hole.
- **B.** Waiting until he has calculated every possible danger.
- **C.** Leaving all repairs to people outside the station.
- **D.** Recording his thoughts before responding.

**4.3. What is implied by the final thought that, had events gone differently, he might no longer be thinking?**

- **A.** He expected to forget the incident once he went home.
- **B.** He believed thinking itself had caused the emergency.
- **C.** He planned to stop considering the risks of future flights.
- **D.** He realizes that a different outcome could have cost him his life.

**Answer key and evidence**

**4.1: B — sequence**

Track the shift from immediate action to later reflection rather than attaching fear to the opening hypothetical alone.

- c7 · 00:16.52–00:21.24: «то там кидаешься пожар тушить — ты даже не думаешь, насколько это опасно —»
- c9 · 00:25.20–00:28.31: «А потом, когда ты осмысливаешь ситуацию,»
- c10 · 00:28.92–00:31.97: «уже вроде всё улеглось, и тебе становится потом страшно,»

- **A:** He says he acts during the emergency without thinking about how dangerous it is.
- **B:** The later reflection follows the urgent response and the situation settling down.
- **C:** No outside explanation is required in his account.
- **D:** The opening is hypothetical; the main example concerns reflection after emergencies.

**4.2: A — explicit_detail**

The item tests the named actions, not technical knowledge of spacecraft repairs.

- c7 · 00:16.52–00:21.24: «то там кидаешься пожар тушить — ты даже не думаешь, насколько это опасно —»
- c8 · 00:21.54–00:24.54: «или дырочку закрываешь.»

- **A:** These are the two concrete responses he names.
- **B:** He emphasizes acting without dwelling on the danger.
- **C:** No such division of responsibility is described.
- **D:** Reflection is described as following action, not preceding it.

**4.3: D — supported_inference**

The phrase is indirect. The context supports possible death, not a specific injury or a claim that the actual event was fatal.

- c10 · 00:28.92–00:31.97: «уже вроде всё улеглось, и тебе становится потом страшно,»
- c11 · 00:31.99–00:34.78: «думаешь: ёлки-палки, вот если бы я бы вот...»
- c12 · 00:34.81–00:38.06: «по-другому пошло бы всё, то я бы уже, может быть, и не думал бы.»

- **A:** The counterfactual concerns what could have happened during the dangerous event, not later memory.
- **B:** He never makes that causal claim.
- **C:** The passage does not state a future decision of that kind.
- **D:** The euphemistic ending follows reflection on a dangerous emergency and supports this limited inference.

### 5. Андрис Лиепа — Arts / family expectations

Recording date: 2009-12-01 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:39.77 · target from transcript: 2+

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Andris_Liepa_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/Andris_Liepa_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=631766019)

**Why this level:** The account moves from concrete parental occupations to family expectations and an idiomatic saying whose applicability is challenged. This is a stretch selection rather than an easy biographical clip.

**Teacher listening check:** Listen for whether the proverb and the final qualification are clear. The proverb’s meaning must be inferred from this family context; do not require prior knowledge of the speaker’s biography.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

Всегда старался делать всё не на 100, а на 200 %. Я понимал, что родился не в простой семье. Мама – актриса замечательная драматическая. Папа – величайший танцовщик 20 века. И, конечно, надо было как-то соответствовать уровню творческого какого-то потенциала семьи. И часто говорят, что природа на детях отдыхает. И это очень часто в нашей семье звучало. И сейчас приятные какие-то впечатления от тех людей, которые смотрят за нами, и видят, и говорят, что это изречение не совсем правильное в формате Вашей семьи.

**Questions**

**5.1. Why does he say he tried to give more than an ordinary effort?**

- **A.** His parents had discouraged him from entering the arts.
- **B.** He was required to work twice as many hours as his colleagues.
- **C.** He felt he needed to live up to his family’s artistic achievements.
- **D.** He wanted to show that artistic success requires no family influence.

**5.2. In this context, what idea is expressed by the saying that “nature rests on the children”?**

- **A.** Children of artists need more rest than their parents.
- **B.** Children of gifted parents may fail to match their parents’ talents.
- **C.** Artistic talent develops only when children live close to nature.
- **D.** Parents should leave all career decisions to their children.

**5.3. What reaction from observers does he find gratifying?**

- **A.** They believe his family should leave the performing arts.
- **B.** They say his parents’ achievements were exaggerated.
- **C.** They agree that the saying accurately describes his family.
- **D.** They suggest that his family is an exception to the saying.

**Answer key and evidence**

**5.1: C — motivation**

Connect the exaggerated percentage to the reason he gives, rather than treating it as a measurable workload.

- c1 · 00:00.00–00:03.95: «Всегда старался делать всё не на 100, а на 200 %.»
- c2 · 00:04.18–00:06.72: «Я понимал, что родился не в простой семье.»
- c3 · 00:07.63–00:09.80: «Мама – актриса замечательная драматическая.»
- c4 · 00:09.85–00:14.27: «Папа – величайший танцовщик 20 века.»
- c5 · 00:14.32–00:18.52: «И, конечно, надо было как-то соответствовать уровню»
- c6 · 00:18.55–00:21.05: «творческого какого-то потенциала семьи.»

- **A:** He describes their achievements and his wish to meet the family standard, not discouragement.
- **B:** The percentage is rhetorical effort, not a literal work schedule.
- **C:** The descriptions of his parents lead directly to the need to match the family’s creative level.
- **D:** That general claim is not made.

**5.2: B — idiom_in_context**

Use the surrounding account of a talented family to interpret the figurative expression.

- c3 · 00:07.63–00:09.80: «Мама – актриса замечательная драматическая.»
- c4 · 00:09.85–00:14.27: «Папа – величайший танцовщик 20 века.»
- c5 · 00:14.32–00:18.52: «И, конечно, надо было как-то соответствовать уровню»
- c6 · 00:18.55–00:21.05: «творческого какого-то потенциала семьи.»

### 6. Дарья Донцова — Literature / readers’ expectations

Recording date: 2008-01-30 · whole excerpt: 00:00–00:37.53 · target from transcript: 2+

[Audio source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dar%27ya_Dontsova_voice.oga) · [Direct original audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0c/Dar%27ya_Dontsova_voice.oga) · [Published timed transcript](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=633582204)

**Why this level:** An argumentative answer distinguishes genres and uses ironic recommendations and colloquial wording. Understanding the speaker’s position is more demanding than recognizing the listed genres.

**Teacher listening check:** The source includes the colloquial disparaging phrase паршивые дюдики and the rhetorical что делать. Neither should become an isolated vocabulary test. Confirm delivery and nuance by listening before approval.

**Published transcript (line breaks joined):**

Вопрос по поводу того, не противно ли Вам писать паршивые дюдики, задают только журналисты в России. Потому что западные журналисты как-то очень хорошо понимают, что литература состоит из жанров. И в каждом жанре есть свои условия. Есть детектив, есть экшн, есть, предположим, философский роман. Если Вы хотите узнать какие-нибудь знания по географии — бога ради, не читайте госпожу Донцову. Если Вы хотите получить ответ на сакральный вопрос: что делать? Ну, тоже я не для Вас. Надо понимать, для чего ты берёшь какую книгу в руки.

**Questions**

**6.1. What is the main point of the speaker’s response?**

- **A.** Books should be approached with expectations suited to their genre and purpose.
- **B.** Every successful novel should teach geography and answer philosophical questions.
- **C.** Detective fiction should be replaced by philosophical novels.
- **D.** Journalists should stop asking writers about their work altogether.

**6.2. What does she advise someone seeking geographical knowledge from a book?**

- **A.** Choose her books because they provide that knowledge indirectly.
- **B.** Read her detective stories before trying any other genre.
- **C.** Choose something other than her books for that purpose.
- **D.** Avoid reading books and ask a journalist instead.

**6.3. Why, in her account, do Western journalists avoid the particular disparaging question she mentions?**

- **A.** They have not encountered detective fiction before.
- **B.** They understand that different literary genres have different conventions.
- **C.** They believe all books should follow the same literary rules.
- **D.** They are interested only in facts about geography.

**Answer key and evidence**

**6.1: A — main_idea**

The examples support a principle about appropriate expectations, not a rejection of other genres.

- c3 · 00:06.86–00:09.86: «Потому что западные журналисты как-то очень хорошо понимают,»
- c4 · 00:09.89–00:12.03: «что литература состоит из жанров.»
- c5 · 00:12.23–00:14.52: «И в каждом жанре есть свои условия.»
- c6 · 00:14.55–00:20.48: «Есть детектив, есть экшн, есть, предположим, философский роман.»
- c11 · 00:34.10–00:37.56: «Надо понимать, для чего ты берёшь какую книгу в руки.»

- **A:** She distinguishes genres and concludes that readers should understand why they pick up a particular book.
- **B:** She uses those expectations as reasons certain readers should not choose her books.
- **C:** She recognizes different genres rather than proposing that one replace another.
- **D:** Her criticism targets a particular kind of question, not all questions about writing.

**6.2: C — speaker_recommendation**

Understand the negative recommendation within its stated purpose; it is not a claim that nobody should read her.

- c7 · 00:20.52–00:24.22: «Если Вы хотите узнать какие-нибудь знания по географии —»
- c8 · 00:24.25–00:26.63: «бога ради, не читайте госпожу Донцову.»

- **A:** She explicitly says not to read her for that purpose.
- **B:** No such reading sequence is recommended.
- **C:** The conditional example ends with не читайте госпожу Донцову.
- **D:** She does not recommend replacing reading with an interview.

**6.3: B — attributed_explanation**

This is the speaker’s characterization of journalists. The item does not assert it as an independently established cultural fact.

- c2 · 00:04.10–00:06.62: «задают только журналисты в России.»
- c3 · 00:06.86–00:09.86: «Потому что западные журналисты как-то очень хорошо понимают,»
- c4 · 00:09.89–00:12.03: «что литература состоит из жанров.»
- c5 · 00:12.23–00:14.52: «И в каждом жанре есть свои условия.»

- **A:** She credits them with understanding genre distinctions, not ignorance of a genre.
- **B:** Her Потому что explanation connects their behavior to recognizing genres and their conditions.
- **C:** This reverses her explanation of genre differences.
- **D:** Geography is a later example of a reader’s purpose, not an explanation of journalists’ behavior.

## Approval rubric

1. Listen at normal speed without the transcript first. Judge whether the excerpt is self-contained and the questions are answerable from audio alone.
2. Compare every caption with the actual audio. Record omissions, extra words, uncertain words, and mismatched boundaries. Source publication does not guarantee verbatim accuracy.
3. Rate pace, clarity, accent/register, number of speakers, overlap, background sound, and memory load separately from vocabulary and syntax.
4. Check that each key is uniquely best and supported within the clip. Preserve attribution, negation, conditional meaning, and uncertainty. Do not require biographical or scientific knowledge.
5. Review the item set for cross-question clues and dependence. Score questions together as a passage family for exposure and adaptive selection purposes.
6. Retain speaker and publisher attribution and separate audio/caption licenses. Keep captions and keys hidden until submission in assessment mode. Practice can expose them afterward.
7. Record reviewer, date, corrections, agreed target level, and approval. Only then enable the items in the diagnostic bank. Human approval still does not constitute empirical calibration.
